# Profile source expansion: public evidence

**Research date:** 2026-07-31
**Decision:** Which additional public projects can sharpen a future Factory Pilot
portfolio of 100+ production profiles without making an upstream application,
its data model, or its runtime authoritative?

This is a research-only, public-evidence memo. It changes no product contract,
candidate, registry, dependency, or implementation. The Graph remains the
source of truth: a source can at most inform Factory-owned Page, Domain, Policy,
Flow, Integration, and Experience projections in a Draft; a Published Revision
alone may compile. See the [platform boundary](../architecture/application-graph-platform.md)
and [adoption gate](../ecosystem/open-source-adoption.md).

## Method and terminology

Facts below are observations from the linked upstream release/repository and
its licence file, retrieved 2026-07-31. A tagged release is the proposed fixed
reference where one is published; an upstream default branch is **not** a fixed
reference and must be resolved to an immutable commit before any source study.
Release dates, push activity, GitHub stars, or a visible release list are only
maintenance/adoption signals, never a security or suitability endorsement.

`Direct`, `Selective`, `Provider`, `Reference`, and `Excluded` are the
fixed-reference strategies used in this memo. They have the same meanings as
the 2026-07-30 portfolio: direct and selective still require a governed source
study; provider means a replaceable Factory-owned adapter; reference means
independently authored implementation; excluded means no copying, linking,
embedding, packaging, generated output, or runtime use. **Potentially
permissible** answers only the narrow licence question. It is not approval to
reuse source and does not override trademark, asset, data, dependency, security,
or regulated-domain restrictions.

## Cross-source findings

### Observed facts

1. The strongest maintained permissive references in the missing families are
   HAPI FHIR (Apache-2.0, release 2026-07-22), OpenWMS.org (Apache-2.0, release
   2025-02-22), GraphHopper (Apache-2.0, tagged 11.0), Kill Bill (Apache-2.0,
   release 2026-07-09), Kolibri/Payload/Flarum/MicroRealEstate (MIT), and
   Apache Fineract (Apache-2.0). Their licence links are in the table.
2. CRM, full-LMS, helpdesk, asset, healthcare, work-management, and several
   accounting candidates are AGPL/GPL/custom/unclear. Public availability does
   not create a source-reuse permission, and several repositories have no
   GitHub SPDX classification at all.
3. The landscape makes external-provider boundaries especially concrete for
   routing, route optimisation, subscription billing, and FHIR interoperability:
   each upstream can supply a bounded query/command result without owning
   Factory's business state.
4. Mature full applications routinely bundle migrations, UI, authentication,
   integrations, operational data retention, and background work with their
   vertical business logic. That makes them poor direct starting points for an
   Application Graph platform even where their licence is permissive.

### Factory inferences and the product decisions affected

| Product decision | Evidence-supported inference | Profiles that could benefit later (not implemented) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep `party`, `activity`, `assignment`, `deadline`, `case`, and audit independent of a CRM vendor. | CRM sources span leads/opportunities/cases/relationship follow-up but are AGPL/custom. Independent shared primitives preserve reuse across sales, support, property, and internal work. | Lead capture, opportunity pipeline, account success, partner onboarding, customer support, incident command. |
| Make reservation/capacity a native contract, not an appointment or ticketing application import. | LibreBooking is GPL and ticketing sources are GPL/unclear; all expose variations of resource/time/capacity/participant/check-in. | Appointment booking, room/equipment reservation, class registration, concerts, workshops, waitlists. |
| Treat health integrations as regulated provider adapters. | HAPI FHIR is permissively licensed but operates in an interoperability domain; Bahmni is AGPL and clinical. | Clinic administrative appointment, referral tracking, consent registry, medical-billing administration. |
| Split warehouse/asset/field work into movement, custody, work order, route, and proof-of-completion capabilities. | OpenWMS, Snipe-IT, GraphHopper, and jsprit separate these concerns in different ways. | Receiving, pick-pack-ship, asset checkout, technician scheduling, dispatch exceptions, proof of delivery. |
| Keep money movement and accounting kernels Factory-owned and specialist-reviewed. | Kill Bill and Fineract are permissive but high-risk full runtimes; Akaunting is unclear and Firefly is AGPL. | Invoice, AR collection, vendor-bill approval, reimbursement, period close, credit-note workflow. |
| Separate content, knowledge, feedback, and operational work rather than selecting a single back-office platform. | Payload/Flarum are permissive references; Plane/iTop/NocoDB/Directus have reciprocal or unclear terms. | Help center, SOP library, CMS pages, feedback intake, facilities queue, procurement, change management. |

## Promotion requirements and current limits

No strategy in this report authorizes reuse. Before a candidate can move from
reference to an approved Factory component or provider, attach an immutable
tag-to-SHA record; exact paths and line ranges; licence/trademark/notice review;
SBOM, provenance, security, secret, malware, and vulnerability results; and
Factory-owned contracts, fixtures, semantic validation, compiler tests, provider
conformance tests, failure tests, and removal path. No adapter may mutate a
Published Revision, bypass policy/audit, persist raw AI material, or make an
external system's data model authoritative.

In particular, payment, accounting, identity, location, healthcare, and route
optimisation need their own specialist reviews. Public releases, star counts,
or active repositories do not clear those gates.

## Portfolio effect

This adds **28 source records** to the earlier 43-candidate landscape without
claiming any capability is implemented. It increases coverage in CRM/sales (3),
booking/ticketing (3), LMS (3), healthcare administration (2),
warehouse/field/logistics (5), property (1), accounting/invoicing (4), and
support/knowledge/CMS/internal operations (7). The likely near-term value is
not importing any full application: it is independently specifying reusable
shared seams so future profiles can be assembled from immutable Graph
capabilities and optional, replaceable providers.
